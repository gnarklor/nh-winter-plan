    Why it matters

    The reasons behind each part of the checklist, with links to the original sources.

    Winter is the main risk

    The 2008 ice storm cut power to about 440,000 New Hampshire customers, some for nearly two weeks. That's why the target is two weeks, not three days. (WMUR)

    Ready.gov's winter guide covers heating, driving and staying warm when the power goes out. (Ready.gov Winter, Ready.gov basic kit)

    Water

    The CDC recommends 1 gallon per person per day: 14 gallons for two weeks. If that isn't possible yet, 3 gallons is the minimum. Replace stored water every 6 months. (CDC water storage)

    A private well needs electricity for its pump, so a power outage also means no running water. Many homes in towns like Antrim are on private wells. (NEHA well facts, Town of Antrim)

    If you're unsure about water, boil it for 1 minute or add 8 drops of plain unscented bleach (5–9% sodium hypochlorite) per gallon. Double the bleach if the water is cloudy or very cold, then wait 30 minutes. Boiling and bleach won't remove fuel or chemicals. (CDC emergency water)

    Why there are no rain barrels: they freeze in a NH winter, and roof runoff can carry germs and chemicals. (CDC rainwater)

    Food

    A fridge keeps food safe for about 4 hours without power. A full freezer lasts about 48 hours, or 24 if it's half full. After that, you'll need food that doesn't need cooking or a fridge: about 14 cans of protein, 14 cans of fruit or vegetables, peanut butter and breakfast items per person. That costs roughly $45–75 per person, and you can spread it out over several grocery trips. (CDC power outage)

    Warmth, light and power

    The cheapest heat is what you're wearing. Gather everyone into one room, wear layers and use blankets. Headlamps are safer than candles. A charged power bank keeps a phone working for alerts and calls.

    Carbon monoxide (CO) has no smell and kills people every winter during outages. Never use a generator, grill, camp stove or gas oven to heat indoors. Generators must be at least 20 feet from windows, doors and vents. (NH DHHS on CO, CDC)

    Plugging a generator into a wall outlet without a transfer switch sends power back into the lines, which can kill line workers. (NH Fire Marshal bulletin)

    Medicine and papers

    In 2024, a ransomware attack on Change Healthcare delayed prescriptions at pharmacies across the US. Keeping at least 7 extra days of medicine on hand and a paper medication list means a computer outage doesn't become a medical emergency. (TechCrunch)

    Medicine that needs refrigeration should generally be replaced after a day or more without power. If a medicine is life-sustaining, keep using it until you can get a replacement. (CDC)

    Card readers and ATMs stop working when the power or network is down, so keep $40–100 in small bills if you can.

    Cyber attacks: what's actually at risk

    • Iran: after the war that began in February 2026, Iran-linked hackers went after control equipment used by US water utilities and other infrastructure. In July, more than 100 US water systems were targeted, and one Georgia town issued a boil-water advisory. (CISA AA26-097A, TechCrunch, CBS News, CSIS)
    • Russia: pro-Russia hackers caused a water tank in Texas to overflow, and another attack cut water to about 450 homes in Denmark. (GovInfoSecurity, Meduza/Bloomberg)
    • China: the Volt Typhoon group spent about 300 days inside a small Massachusetts utility's network, just over the NH border. (SecurityWeek)
    • North Korea mostly steals money, especially cryptocurrency. For families, that shows up as scams, fake job offers and phishing. (BleepingComputer)
    • Phones: after hackers broke into US phone carriers, the FBI advised using encrypted messaging apps. The FBI also warns that criminals use AI to fake a relative's voice, which is why a family code word helps. (CAI/NPR, FBI IC3)

    What helps at home is the same four habits CISA recommends: strong passwords, multifactor authentication, software updates and recognizing phishing. (CISA Secure Our World)

    Help paying for it

    • Fuel Assistance: grants of $188–$2,500 for heating fuel. Apply through your Community Action Agency or call 211. (NH Dept. of Energy)
    • Electric Assistance Program: a 5–86% discount on electric bills for Eversource, Unitil, Liberty and NHEC customers. (NH EAP)
    • Community Action Agency phone numbers for every county. (NH Dept. of Energy list)
    • NH Alerts sign-up, through the Genasys Protect app. (ReadyNH)
    • NOAA weather radio stations in NH. (weather.gov)
